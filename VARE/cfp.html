General information

In VAR-E 2027, we consider the scope of immersive environments to be broad and invite innovative and fundamental research across a wide spectrum of virtual environments. Specifically, any type of environment in the reality-virtuality spectrum is a virtual environment and any system that embodies the interplay across humans, computing devices, interaction technologies, and feedback mechanisms is a virtual system.

We invite you to submit your research at VARE 2027 in the form of:

Technical Paper

A full-length, peer-reviewed paper that is accepted for presentation at a technical session and publication in the conference proceedings. It requires a draft paper upon submittal for consideration; if accepted, a final paper is required. Templates and guidelines can be found here. A research paper, if accepted, will be published in the proceeding of the conference (indexed by Scopus). Selected papers will be considered for extension as a journal publication to be submitted for consideration in The ASME Journal of Computing and Information Science in Engineering (JCISE). Moreover, one of the paper submitted to VARE will be awarded with the "VAR-E Best Paper Award".

Technical Presentation

A non-publication presentation that is accepted and scheduled for presentation at a technical session but is not published in the conference proceedings. It only requires an abstract upon submittal for consideration. This is an excellent way to disseminate project results, already published research, etc...

We remind you that one fee covers up to two presentation slots at the conference, which can include two technical papers, two technical presentations, or a mix of the two.

Topics

VAR-E 2027 invites submissions across four tracks. A paper may fit more than one; submit it to whichever describes it best.

Track 01

Enabling and intelligent technologies for extended realities

This track focuses on the enabling technologies that support virtual, augmented, and mixed reality systems, from the acquisition and representation of the physical world to the generation and delivery of interactive immersive experiences. It welcomes contributions addressing the technological foundations of these systems, including advances in rendering, 3D capture and reconstruction, tracking and registration, displays and haptics, as well as software frameworks and architectures for local, remote, and cloud-based applications. The track also covers emerging computational technologies that extend the capabilities of immersive systems. Artificial intelligence and machine learning are an increasingly important part of this landscape, enabling new approaches to content creation, perception, adaptive interaction, and authoring. Recent developments in generative AI and multimodal models further enable conversational interfaces and intelligent agents, while virtual environments can provide controlled settings and synthetic data for training and evaluating embodied AI and robotic systems. Contributions addressing performance, reliability, and rigorous evaluation are also encouraged, including research on the specific challenges introduced by AI-enabled approaches. Work supporting reproducibility through benchmarks, shared datasets, testbeds, and systematic performance characterization is particularly welcome. Theoretical studies, systems papers, case studies, position papers, and review papers are all within the scope of the track.

Topics of interest

  • Large language and vision-language models as XR interfaces and agents
  • Generative AI for 3D content, scene, and environment authoring
  • 3D reconstruction and scene capture (photogrammetry, NeRF, Gaussian splatting)
  • Rendering pipelines, remote and cloud rendering, and WebXR
  • Tracking, registration, calibration, and spatial mapping
  • Display, optics, passthrough mixed-reality, and haptic hardware
  • Development frameworks, engines, and authoring toolkits
  • Synthetic data generation from virtual environments for perception and control
  • Embodied AI, VR teleoperation, and learning from demonstration
  • Scene understanding, object recognition, and semantic 3D perception
  • Datasets, benchmarks, testbeds, and reproducibility in XR research
  • System performance, latency, and fidelity characterization
  • Evaluation, reliability, grounding, and responsible use of AI in immersive systems

Track 02

Human factors, user experience, and interaction in XR

Extended-reality systems are only as good as the interaction and communication they establish with their users. The design of virtual experiences rests on an understanding of human perception, cognition, behavior, and action, and on the systematic study of interaction techniques, spatial input technologies, and the visual and non-visual representation of information. This track seeks contributions that enrich user experience in virtual environments through the development and evaluation of (a) new ways to interact with virtual objects, (b) new ways to interact with other people in virtual and hybrid spaces, (c) new ways to represent geometric and non-geometric information, and (d) new insight into how users behave and perform in virtual environments. Work on defining, measuring, and reporting user experience in cyber-physical environments is also of interest, as is research on accessibility and inclusive design, which the track treats as a first-class concern rather than a special case.

Topics of interest

  • Collaborative and multi-user virtual environments
  • Spatial, multi-touch, gesture, and micro-gesture interaction
  • Speech-based and multimodal interaction
  • Haptic- and gaze-based interaction in VR/AR/MR
  • Mobile, wearable, and tangible interaction technologies
  • Physiological and behavioral sensing (EEG, ECG, eye tracking) in virtual environments
  • Visualization of geometric and non-geometric data; immersive analytics
  • Presence, cybersickness, comfort, workload, and trust
  • Accessibility, disability, and inclusive design for XR
  • Adaptive and personalized immersive experiences, including AI-mediated interaction
  • Evaluation methodology, metrics, and measurement of user performance
  • Ethics, privacy, and data protection in immersive systems

Track 03

XR applications in engineering, manufacturing, and education

Immersive interfaces have become pervasive across design, manufacturing, inspection, customization, education and technical training, and the level of immersion available along the reality–virtuality continuum changes how designers design, how engineers produce, and how users use products. This track invites research that puts extended-reality technology to work in a concrete engineering context and reports evidence of what it achieves there. Contributions include assembly and maintenance support, inspection and quality control, human-robot collaboration, digital twins and industrial cyber-physical systems, virtual commissioning, and immersive analysis of engineering and simulation data. Education and training contributions include immersive classrooms, workforce and safety training, and remote or distributed instruction. Deployment experience, field studies, industrial case studies and honest negative results are as welcome as new systems; papers reporting competence, performance, usability or ergonomic assessment of a deployed system are strongly encouraged.

Topics of interest

  • AR/VR-assisted assembly, disassembly, inspection, diagnosis, and maintenance
  • XR and digital twins; industrial cyber-physical systems and IIoT
  • Virtual commissioning and simulation-driven production systems
  • Human-machine and human-robot collaboration in immersive settings
  • Virtual environments for engineering design, design review, and decision support
  • Immersive analytics of engineering, simulation, and sensor data
  • XR for education, virtual classrooms, and remote instruction
  • Immersive workforce, safety, and technical training
  • Multi-user and distributed XR systems in collaborative workspaces
  • Competence, performance, and usability assessment of deployed XR systems
  • Ergonomic analysis of XR systems and workplaces
  • Field deployment, industrial case studies, and lessons learned

Track 04

Extended reality and immersive visualization for healthcare

This track covers extended reality and immersive visualization technologies applied to clinical practice, medical education, patient care, and healthcare engineering. Contributions are invited on surgical planning, simulation, and intraoperative guidance; VR and AR for clinical skills training and competency assessment; immersive visualization of medical imaging and patient data; patient-specific anatomical models and clinical digital twins; rehabilitation, therapy, pain management and assistive immersive systems; and the design and evaluation of medical devices and clinical workflows evaluated in virtual environments. Work addressing usability, accessibility, safety, human factors, AI-enabled XR, technical validation, and clinical evaluation is also encouraged. Submissions must make a substantive contribution involving extended reality, immersive visualization, or interactive 3D environments. Technical validation, phantom and cadaveric evaluations, usability studies, rigorous clinical pilot studies and negative results are explicitly welcome.

Topics of interest

  • Surgical planning, simulation, and intraoperative AR guidance
  • Immersive visualization of volumetric medical imaging (CT, MRI, ultrasound)
  • Patient-specific anatomical models and clinical digital twins
  • VR/AR clinical skills training and competency assessment
  • Rehabilitation, therapy, pain management, and exposure therapy
  • Accessible, assistive, and patient-centered immersive systems
  • Medical device design and clinical workflow evaluation in virtual environments
  • Registration accuracy, validation methodology, and clinical evidence
